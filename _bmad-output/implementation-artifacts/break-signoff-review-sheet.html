What changed since your second pass

You queried the four remaining per-character CJK items. Together with the three from the first pass, that was all seven — so I researched it rather than making you adjudicate a typographic standard.

You were right, and so was the engine. The Unicode line-breaking standard says a line may ordinarily break between any two ideographic characters, and the W3C’s Chinese and Japanese layout requirements both regulate only where breaks are forbidden — neither asks for word-aware breaking. So breaking inside 汉字 is permitted by the standard, and the engine is behaving correctly. What you were expressing is a different thing: that these particular values are units. That is a document-level fact, and the engine has a channel for exactly it. All seven now carry that declaration.

One new row, and its label is not yours. Declaring all seven would have left nothing in the fixture exercising how the engine actually breaks CJK. So cjk-008 is an undeclared eighteen-character run, taken from text already in another fixture rather than invented. Its label follows from the Unicode standard, not from a native reader, and it is marked derived throughout — your sign-off does not certify it.
