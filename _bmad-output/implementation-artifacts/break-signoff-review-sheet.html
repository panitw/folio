What changed since your first pass

Your four corrections split into two kinds, and they were handled differently because they point in opposite directions.

You wanted fewer breaks in 北京. That is expressible: the engine has a channel for “this value is declared, never split it”, built because Thai surnames are made of ordinary dictionary words and no rule can separate them. 北京 now carries that declaration — and so do 东京都 and こんにちは, which you added when I showed you their actual break positions. The engine produces no break there because it was told, not because it inferred anything.

You wanted more breaks in the three Thai compounds. Nothing can express that. A declaration can only remove break opportunities, never add them. Removing those words from the shipped dictionary would make it factually wrong and silently change every document containing them; inferring where a compound may split is what the architecture already rules impossible. So they are recorded as known divergences: the fixture keeps your judgment, the engine falls short of it, and the shortfall is stated in the format spec rather than hidden.

One consequence worth knowing: the fixture used to claim its labels followed a rule — if a word is in the dictionary, never break inside it. All three compounds you split are in the dictionary, and so are the five you kept whole. That rule could never have predicted your judgment, so it has been replaced with an admission that no such rule exists.
